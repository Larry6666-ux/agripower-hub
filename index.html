The Problem

A fragmented infrastructure problem — not a farming problem

We did not read this in a report. We sat with more than 25 smallholder farmers in Edo State and counted their costs, line by line.

🛢️

Diesel eats the margin

Diesel-powered processing consumes 40–60% of a farmer's processing income — and price swings erase what is left.

📏

Distance kills the crop

The nearest mechanised processors sit 20–30 km away. Fresh cassava perishes within 2–3 days, forcing distress sales to middlemen.

⚙️

Equipment is out of reach

A grater–press set is unaffordable for a smallholder household, so farmers never escape the diesel-mill dependency.

👩🏾‍🌾

Women carry the load

Women do most of the processing — and the travel, and the waiting. They pay the highest cost per naira earned.

The Model

Pay As You Process

A solar-powered hub inside the village. Farmers bring their cassava and pay a small fee per kilogram — like the pay-as-you-go they already use for phone credit.

1 · HARVESTfarmer brings fresh cassava to the hub
2 · WEIGH & BOOKposted per-kg tariff, every kilo recorded
3 · PROCESSwashed, grated, pressed — solar powered
4 · MARKETtransparent prices, access to verified buyers
5 · EARNfarmer keeps the product — and the margin

The Pilot

Evbuwa, Edo State — scoped and engineered

Our first production hub is scoped for Evbuwa community — 2 tonnes/day design capacity, connected to the community mini-grid, so every tonne processed is also the daytime electricity demand that keeps rural grids alive.

Honest cost

A full commercial hub is a ₦30–40M investment (mini-grid powered). We deploy in phases, proving utilisation before we scale.

Built to replicate

The model is documented as an open playbook so farmer cooperatives can operate hubs — infrastructure designed to outgrow its founder.

Measured from day one

Energy cost per tonne, farmers served, income change, diesel displaced — our first GRI-structured impact report is in production.
