The Challenge

A fragmented infrastructure problem — not a farming problem.

We did not read this in a report. We sat with more than 25 smallholder farmers in Edo State and counted their costs, line by line.

🛢️

Diesel eats the margin

Diesel-powered processing consumes 40–60% of a farmer's processing income — and price swings erase what is left.

📏

Distance kills the crop

The nearest mechanised processors sit 20–30 km away. Fresh cassava perishes within 2–3 days, forcing distress sales to middlemen.

⚙️

Equipment is out of reach

A grater–press set is unaffordable for a smallholder household, so farmers never escape the diesel-mill dependency.

👩🏾‍🌾

Women carry the load

Women do most of the processing — and the travel, and the waiting. They pay the highest cost per naira earned.

The Model

Pay As You Process

A solar-powered hub inside the village. Farmers bring their cassava and pay a small fee per kilogram — like the pay-as-you-go they already use for phone credit. No machines to buy. No diesel to gamble on.

HarvestFarmer brings fresh cassava to the hub — at walking distance, not 30 km away.
Weigh & BookPosted per-kg tariff. Every kilogram recorded — trust by design.
ProcessWashed, grated, pressed — powered by clean energy, run by trained local youth.
MarketTransparent prices and access to verified buyers replace the middleman's margin.
EarnThe farmer keeps the product — and keeps the margin.
Every hub doubles as the anchor customer for the community mini-grid — the daytime processing load that keeps rural clean power economically alive.
